Cladding, Flooring & Trim

Interior Finishings

The finishings are what make a van feel like somewhere you actually want to be. Caolán treats this as seriously as any other part of the build — no shortcuts, no cheap materials.

Detail That Lasts

The Difference Between
Good and Great

The mechanical side of a build keeps you warm and powers your life. The finishings are what make you actually enjoy being in the space. Cladding, flooring, headliner, upholstery, trim — these are the details that separate a functional build from a beautiful one.

Caolán chooses materials that look premium but can take the reality of van life — damp gear, muddy boots, condensation on winter mornings. He won't use materials that look great in a showroom and fall apart in six months. He lives in a van. He knows the difference.

"Every material choice in a build is a trade-off between look, weight, and durability. I'll tell you what that trade-off is before we decide."

Finishing Options

Wall Cladding

The walls set the character of the van interior. Caolán works with a few different materials depending on the look you're after — warm and natural, clean and modern, or somewhere in between.

Tongue & Groove Classic van life look — warm, natural, and light. Pine or hardwood. Caolán's personal choice.
Ply Lining Smooth birch ply — contemporary, clean, and easy to paint or varnish any colour you want.
Composite Panel Lightweight, durable, wipe-clean. Good for a more modern or commercial look.

Flooring

Van flooring needs to handle mud, water, and heavy gear without looking rough after two months. Caolán fits over a properly levelled ply sub-floor — never straight onto the van metal.

Vinyl Plank Waterproof, durable, and looks great in wood or stone effect. Caolán's most common recommendation.
Real Wood Oak or bamboo hardwood. Beautiful, but needs more care and doesn't love sustained dampness.
Rubber / Anti-Slip Ideal for garage areas, rear sections, or vans used for sport and outdoor gear.

Headliner & Ceiling

The ceiling finish is what most people forget about until it's done — and then they can't imagine the van without it. It also hides the insulation and any wiring runs above the cladding.

Carpet Lining Warm, soft, no condensation on the surface, great for sound absorption. Very practical for Irish conditions.
Ply / Timber Curved ply ceiling for a more architectural, finished feel. Takes more time but the result is worth it.
PVC Headliner Wipe-clean, modern, lightweight. Common in commercial applications or budget-conscious builds.

Lighting

Good lighting changes everything about how liveable a van feels. Caolán plans lighting zones — task lighting, ambient, reading light by the bed — rather than just dotting LEDs around the ceiling.

LED Spotlights Recessed into the ceiling or cladding. Warm white for ambience, cool white over a kitchen worktop.
Strip Lighting Under-cabinet, under-bed, or behind trim for indirect lighting. Creates a premium feel.
Dimmable Controls All circuits can be put on dimmers — proper wired dimmers, not cheap inline ones.

Insulation First.
Always.

Insulation is invisible in a finished van — but it's the difference between a van that's comfortable in January and one that you dread sleeping in. Caolán insulates before he clads, properly, with no cold bridges left untreated.

He's obsessive about it. Because he's lived in a van through Irish winters and knows exactly what inadequate insulation feels like at 3am in a Connemara car park.

Spray Foam

Fills every gap and contour — great for complex body panels and eliminating cold bridges at ribs and joints.

Rigid Foam Board

High R-value, easy to cut to shape, good for floors and large flat panels. Caolán's main wall insulator.

Thinsulate / Acoustic

Fibrous insulation for awkward cavities and door cards — excellent sound damping as a bonus.

Vapour Control

Managing moisture properly is as important as the insulation itself. Condensation rot is real — Caolán accounts for it.
